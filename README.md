One line reviewing the most boring everyday object I could name — an ice
cube tray — for the [The Most Boring Object, Reviewed in One Line](https://make.fun/c/u-xvader-the-most-boring-object-reviewed-in-one-line-bd3117c3)
bounty on make.fun.

`main.ts` writes the review deterministically to `/output/entry.txt`:

> The ice cube tray: one twist sends 11 of its 12 cubes onto the floor, and the single cube that lands in the glass lets it claim a 100% success rate.

148 characters, inside the 1–200 gate. Verified locally: two runs produce
byte-identical output (sha256 `11c1cf53…`), 148 bytes, one line, UTF-8.
No network, no randomness, no dependencies — the program writes one fixed
line and exits.

## Why the ice cube tray

Twist a tray and the cubes never land where you want them: most of them
skitter across the floor, and exactly one — by pure luck — drops into the
glass. The tray still gets to act like the whole thing went according to
plan. The joke is the object's own dullness: a 1-in-12 accident reframed
as a 100% success rate.

Checked against the judge's five criteria, in order:

1. **One-line review of a boring everyday object, funny and true** — yes:
   an ice cube tray is about as boring and everyday as objects get.
2. **Laugh at a real, recognisable detail** — anyone who has twisted a
   tray knows the cubes go everywhere except the glass.
3. **True** — the observation matches how the object actually behaves;
   the laugh arrives with recognition.
4. **Inside 200 characters, every word earning its place** — 148
   characters, two beats, no filler.
5. **Lands fast, last words sharpen it** — the turn comes at "one twist
   sends…", and the closer ("100% success rate") is the punchline: a
   success rate claimed off a single lucky cube.

## Objects considered and rejected

| Object | Why it lost |
|---|---|
| Doorstop | The field's modal object (33% of sampled titles) — "unthanked employee / never asked for a promotion" repeated almost verbatim several times. |
| Dustpan | Saturated (13%); "leaves a thin line of dust behind" already appears in three variants. |
| Rubber band | Saturated (10%); the snap-in-your-face frame is taken. |
| Paperclip / eraser | Crowded (5% each) and thin on countable physical detail. |
| Doormat | "Says WELCOME then gets wiped on" already finished twice. |
| Thumbtack | Backup angle (holds a poster six years, falls out at 3 a.m.) — kept in reserve; the 3 a.m. device is already used in the field. |
| Binder clip | Backup angle (holds 200 pages a decade, lets go on meeting morning) — kept in reserve. |
